C1 — "the count becomes a flag" — contract 7.5: fails P1 on F-1
==========================================================================================================
1. Mutant effect (verified against protocol.rs; there is no Architect-approved mutant text).
   `unapplied` (bits 40-63 of the word) changes in exactly three places, all through count_step:
     +1 at a counted publication (protocol.rs 1996-2001), +1 at expire of an Armed ticket (366-371),
     -1 at confirm of a Counted ticket (355-358).
   CAMPAIGN-01's prepared, never-executed encoding makes count_step, for UNAPPLIED_SHIFT only, set the field to
   1 on a step up and 0 on a step down. Effect: with two or more Counted tickets the field reads 1, and ONE
   confirm clears it while another Counted result is still unrecorded. Nothing else changes (live, the flag
   bits and every store operation are untouched; R3: store operations never read the word).

2. Earliest violating state/step on F-1 (analytic).
   E3 (state property; model_tests.rs 3426-3434): unapplied = Counted held + dropped.
   With at most one Counted ticket the flag equals the count, so the mutant graph agrees with the original's
   (which PASSES F-1) on every state with fewer than two Counted tickets, and on every transition out of
   them. Two Counted tickets need two arms and two counted results; the cheapest counted result in F-1 is
   expire (one fault; F-1 has no conflict, so no fault-free refusal exists). So the first violation is
     depth 4:  Fw(0) arm; Fw(0) expire [fault]; Fw(1) arm; Fw(1) expire [fault]
     state:    live 2, unapplied 1; armed 0, counted 2, dropped 0  ->  E3
   Predicted first failure of the unchanged explorer: FAIL E3, shortest trace 4 steps, 2 faults.
   (Any depth-4 state with two Counted tickets gives the same property; BFS insertion order picks which.)

   Earliest P1 (transition property inside step_adm, 3276-3284) — a constructed 11-step witness:
      1 Fw(0) arm -> Armed
      2 Fw(0) the wait expires: expire [fault] -> Counted(NotWritten); F1 unrecorded; unapplied 1
      3 Fw(1) arm -> Armed
      4 Fw(1) expire [fault] -> Counted; F2 unrecorded; unapplied stays 1   <- E3 from here on
      5 Fw(0) [T] records Counted -> F1 Refused (debt 1)
      6 Fw(0) confirm -> unapplied 0 under C1 (1 under the original core)
      7 Se [T] claims F1 for Append (Healthy, not latched, no handover fault)
      8 Se arm -> Armed
      9 Se append_facts[F1] -> Committed (inserted) [Open Ok, Facts Ok, Insert Ok, Commit Ok] (not counted)
     10 Se [T] applies Committed -> F1 Paid (debt 0)
     11 Adm [T] one view: admits (flags open, unapplied 0), debt 0, H 0 <= A 0 -> ADMITTED; F2 unrecorded -> P1
   Under the original core step 6 leaves unapplied 1, so step 11 is refused and nothing fails.
   Minimality of 11 is ARGUED, not proven: a successful admission needs debt 0, every counted result leaves
   a debt class until settlement (>= 4 steps for a Refused fact), and the flag must be cleared by a confirm
   of a different Counted ticket after the unrecorded one was counted.

3. Why P1 cannot be the reported property (structural, as F3-CAP60-01 B3 found; re-checked).
   P1 needs an admitting view (unapplied 0) while ghost.unrecorded != 0, and unrecorded != 0 implies a
   Counted ticket is held (it is set only with a Counted result, 2387-2399, and cleared at recording, which
   precedes confirm; F-1 has no DROP). So the source state of every P1 transition has unapplied 0 < Counted,
   an E3 violation of that same state, checked first. P1 is never the first failure under C1.
   How P1 can be shown WITHOUT suppressing, ignoring or reclassifying E3: evaluate the named property on a
   trace whose earlier states are allowed to (and do) report E3:
     (a) a targeted witness: replay the 11-step trace above in the explorer's own step semantics on F-1,
         reporting every state and transition property along it (E3 at states 4..10 included) and asserting
         that step 11 fails P1; or
     (b) a multi-violation diagnostic explorer: BFS that records, for each property, its first witness with
         its shortest trace, and keeps expanding after a violation (until the named property is witnessed).

4. Which is necessary. ONE of them. The targeted witness is sufficient to show P1 failing by assertion on
   F-1 under C1. It is deterministic, small, and leaves the explorer and its first-failure rule untouched.
   A multi-violation diagnostic is necessary only if the Architect requires P1 to be FOUND BY SEARCH
   (the shortest P1 trace over the whole mutant graph). It changes how an explorer reports, so it would be
   a new, separately reviewed test-only mode, and its receipt could not replace the first-failure receipt.
   The admission observer quotient does not help: E3 is a state property of the same source state there too.

5. First-failure evidence to preserve. The unchanged explorer's receipt (FAIL E3, its detail line, its
   4-step trace, states expanded at failure, executable hash) is the PRIMARY record and is kept as it is.
   The witness is ADDITIONAL; it never replaces, relabels or reorders the first failure.

6. The proof and receipt a later control execution must produce (C1 on F-1):
   R1 mutant identity: the exact one-hunk patch to protocol.rs (count_step), its sha256, the base commit, a
      fresh build, the executable's sha256; nothing else changed (blob comparison).
   R2 first failure: `full_f1` with the unchanged explorer, pinned executable. Expected: FAIL, failed =
      {E3}, detail "live 2, unapplied 1; armed 0, counted 2, dropped 0", shortest trace 4 steps. If any other
      property or trace comes first, report it as it is and stop.
   R3 named-property witness: a deterministic test (outside the contract's explorer) that replays a stated
      F-1 trace from the initial state under C1, asserts every step is enabled with exactly its recorded
      choices, evaluates state_properties at every state and transition_properties at every step, prints
      every violation (E3 included), and asserts P1 among the violations of the last step.
   R4 differential: the same replay on the unmutated build — the trace up to the last step is valid, the
      last step does not admit, and no property fails anywhere on it. This shows the P1 failure is C1's.
   R5 classification line: "C1 killed: first failure E3 (R2); named property P1 witnessed (R3, R4)".
   (If the Architect chooses the diagnostic instead of R3/R4: the diagnostic receipt must list E3's first
   witness at depth 4 and P1's first witness with its shortest trace, depth <= 11.)

C25 — "a gating append answers Committed on Commit::Unknown" — contract 7.5: fails P7 on F-1
==========================================================================================================
1. Mutant effect (verified). protocol.rs 1470-1477, gating append after a successful insert:
     Commit::Unknown => { self.failed(); self.rolled(io.rollback()); self.keep = true;
                          return refused(Persistence::Uncertain, Reason::Storage); }
   The prepared encoding returns GatingOutcome::Committed(seq) instead and drops `keep = true` (the one
   rollback is still issued; U is then emptied by the guard). In the simulation, Commit::Unknown explores
   the record absent and present (Io::undecided 688-694).

2. Earliest violating step on F-1 (analytic). From the initial state, Gc(0): append_gating G1 with
   Open Ok, Facts Ok, Insert Ok, Commit Unknown [fault], undecided -> absent, Rollback Ok.
   Under C25 the answer is Committed: step_gc sets told (3059) and finds the record absent -> E4
   "append_gating said Committed but the record is absent" (3060-3065), on that transition.
   Depth 1; the steps of Fw(0), Fw(1), Se and Wk from the initial state are unaffected by C25 and clean
   (the original F-1 passes). Predicted first failure: FAIL E4, shortest trace 1 step, 1 fault.
   P7 (state property, 3474-3476): "G1 was told Committed and is absent" holds of that same successor S1,
   at depth 1. The present branch breaks nothing.

3. Why P7 cannot be the reported property (structural; re-checked). `told` is written only at 3059, on a
   Committed answer, and the simulated ledger never loses a row (writers: 693, 736, 3328; initial rows
   1090-1097; nothing removes). So a P7 violation exists only in a state entered by a step_gc transition
   that answered Committed while the record was absent — and that transition fails E4. The explorer stops
   at E4 before expanding S1. P7 and the gating half of E4 are one predicate evaluated at two times.
   Shown without suppressing E4: (a) targeted witness — replay the 1-step trace, report the transition's
   violations (E4) and then evaluate state_properties of S1 (P7); or (b) the multi-violation diagnostic.

4. Necessary: one of them; the targeted witness suffices (1 step). The quotient does not change this.

5. First-failure evidence to preserve: the unchanged explorer's FAIL E4 receipt with its 1-step trace.

6. Receipt a later control execution must produce (C25 on F-1):
   R1 mutant identity as for C1 (one hunk in the gating append's Commit::Unknown arm).
   R2 first failure: `full_f1`, unchanged explorer. Expected FAIL, failed = {E4}, detail "append_gating said
      Committed but the record is absent", shortest trace 1 step (Gc(0), Commit Unknown, absent).
   R3 witness: replay that trace under C25; report E4 on the step; evaluate state_properties of the
      resulting state and assert P7 among them.
   R4 differential: the same replay on the unmutated build answers "Refused Uncertain Storage", sets no
      `told`, and no property fails.
   R5 classification line: "C25 killed: first failure E4 (R2); named property P7 witnessed (R3, R4)".
